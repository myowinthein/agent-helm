# Design

Help agents avoid generic AI website defaults: repetitive visual treatments, filler sections and product-looking decoration unrelated to the actual content. Select Design explicitly during [adopt](../commands/adopt.html); it is available but unselected by default, independently of Writing, Git and Safety.

## What it asks the agent to do

- Avoid the automatic centered hero and fixed sequence of feature cards, metrics, testimonials and calls to action.
- Avoid nested card surfaces, badge clutter, oversized icon tiles, decorative accent strips and redundant labels.
- Avoid stock gradient/glow packages, automatic headline effects and animation applied to every section.
- Leave out unsupported social proof and fake dashboards or charts that show nothing specific about the product.
- Watch for replacement clichés, including automatically switching to cream-and-serif or dark-and-neon themes.

The rule is a concrete pattern-avoidance guide, rather than a general design course. Its examples describe what agents should avoid introducing by habit. They remain valid choices when explicitly requested, established by the project or grounded in the actual content and interaction.

## Preserve project choices

No font, gradient, card or familiar component is automatically a defect. Avoiding repetition does not require plainness, novelty or replacing a project's design system. Targeted edits preserve the surrounding style and existing functionality, accessibility and responsive behavior.

Keep project branding, tokens, component choices and known user needs in local guidance or the existing design system. Separate local guidance makes reviewed Helm updates easier than customizing the adopted copy. [Writing](writing.html) governs prose; Design addresses repeated visual and structural defaults. Neither rule requires the other.

## Basis and review

The pattern list draws on [Anthropic's frontend observations](https://claude.com/resources/articles/improving-frontend-design-through-skills), its [frontend-design guidance](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md), [Impeccable's catalog](https://impeccable.style/slop/) and [kill-ai-slop](https://github.com/yetone/kill-ai-slop). These are practitioner observations and editorial choices. They do not establish AI authorship, a scientific slop score or universal usability failures.

During a requested review, agents identify the concrete pattern and explain why it is an ungrounded default in that context. Loading the rule starts no redesign, scan, detector installation or approval process. The references are optional background, not required tools or instructions to install external skills.

Checks scale to the change and available tools, while required project checks remain required. Agents report material verification limits and do not claim unperformed checks passed.
